1. Purpose in one paragraph

Paulie's Place is the public 3D lounge where the Yappyverse becomes visible. Visitors open yappyverse.com/lounge and land inside a Seattle-2056 jazz lounge on the 47th floor of a brutalist tower, rain visible through floor-to-ceiling windows, neon in the alley below, holographic art on the walls. The avatars they see are not decorations — they are the actual agents currently working inside PAULIS-PLACE. When the Council debates on the backend, the Council characters at the round booth animate and speak the actual debate. When Pauli rules on a product launch, Pauli slams a gavel on the bar. When Zernio publishes a post, the corresponding avatar walks to the wall of screens and holds up a phone. Everything is voice-controlled — the visitor talks, Jarvis-style, and the nearest avatar responds. There are no dashboards, no SaaS chrome, no data tables. It is a game, a living cutscene, and a business simultaneously.

3. The lounge layout — described exhaustively in words

The lounge is one room, roughly 20m long × 14m wide × 4m ceiling. Origin (0,0,0) at the room's center, floor level. Y is up. Room extends from x=-10 to x=+10, z=-7 to z=+7. Six named zones:

ZONE A — The Bar (north wall)

Position: along z=-6.5, spanning x=-8 to x=+8, height 1.1m. Behind it, a mirrored wall from y=0 to y=3.2, with 12 evenly spaced backlit bottles between x=-5 and x=+5. Behind the mirror at y=1.5m centered, one 2m × 1m embedded screen showing the current top-selling PAULIS-PLACE product. In front of the bar, 6 leather-topped stools at z=-5.5, spaced 1.4m apart from x=-3.5 to x=+3.5. Purpose: the top-of-mind product display and the "Publisher" avatar's home.

ZONE B — The Round Booth (west wall, curved)

Position: centered at (-6.5, 0, 0). Curved leather bench of radius 2.2m spanning 180 degrees facing east. Round dark-wood table diameter 1.8m at seat height 0.75m. Purpose: where the Council sits during debates. Three named seats: Advocate at bearing 60°, Critic at bearing 300°, Judge (Pauli) at the center-back at bearing 0°.

ZONE C — The Window Wall (south, floor to ceiling)

Position: along z=+6.5. Six glass panels 3.2m tall × 2.5m wide each. Behind the glass, the Seattle 2056 skyline — brutalist towers, rain falling continuously (particle system, ~800 particles at any moment), occasional distant lightning every 45–90 seconds. Purpose: world-building. No interactive objects.

ZONE D — The Wall of Screens (east wall)

Position: along x=+9.5, from y=0.5 to y=3.5, spanning z=-5 to z=+5. Twelve 720×1280 vertical screens in a 4-row × 3-column grid. Each screen renders the current state of one of the Yappyverse Zernio accounts (Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube, etc). Purpose: when Zernio publishes, the corresponding screen flashes and updates. The Publisher avatar walks to this wall on lounge.character_posted events.

ZONE E — The Player Piano (northwest corner)

Position: centered at (-8.5, 0, -5.5). A small piano playing continuous ambient jazz-with-synth-bass. Purpose: ambient audio anchor. The camera never focuses here.

ZONE F — The Center Floor (open)

Position: the region roughly (-4 < x < +4, -3 < z < +5). Where avatars walk between zones. Where the visitor stands after the enter animation. Purpose: free movement zone. Contains one holographic table at (0, 0.8, 2) that displays the current product being deliberated on.

5. The scene event → avatar behavior mapping

The lounge listens to a WebSocket at wss://api.paulisplace.dev/lounge/<visitor-session-id>. Every incoming message is a SceneEvent:

SceneEvent:
  event_id:    string
  type:        string          # e.g. "council.debate_start", "council.advocate_speaks"
  character:   string or null  # character-id, when the event is character-scoped
  payload:     object          # event-specific
  duration_ms: integer         # how long this event's animation runs

The event → behavior table:

Event typeBehavior
council.debate_startCamera pushes to Zone B. All three council avatars turn toward the table. Holo table at (0, 0.8, 2) lights up with the topic.
council.advocate_speaksAdvocate character animates speaking, gestures. ElevenLabs stream plays. Lip-sync driven by amplitude envelope of the stream.

council.judge_rulesPauli stands, walks to the bar (Zone A), grabs a small brass bell, taps it once. Sound plays. On-screen text (Zone D center screen) reads the ruling.
lounge.character_postedPublisher avatar walks from idle position to Zone D, faces the screen matching the platform in the event payload, raises phone. Screen flashes and updates with the post preview.

6. Real expression — the animation stack

Each character has three animation layers that blend continuously:

  1. Body layer — one of idle | walking | speaking | attacking | presenting | attentive | sitting | drinking. Cross-fades in 0.35s.
  2. Face layer — driven by an emotional intent field on every scene event: neutral | curious | confident | frustrated | delighted | conspiratorial. Blendshape weights mapped in icm/context/FACE_BLENDSHAPES.md. Cross-fades in 0.5s.
  3. Lip layer — real-time amplitude-driven when an ElevenLabs stream is playing. Otherwise, closed-mouth. Recomputed every audio frame (~20ms).

7. Blender pipeline for new characters

New characters follow the pipeline already established in YAPPYVERSE-FACTORY:

  1. Reference image goes into assets/<id>/reference/.
  2. TripoSR (or Replicate fallback) generates the mesh into assets/<id>/mesh/.
  3. Auto-rig via scripts/auto_rig.py using Blender 5.0 headless. Rig type Rigify. Custom bones for hat/chain/cigar as declared in CHARACTER_CONFIG.
  4. Bake standard animation set (idle, walking, speaking, attacking, presenting, attentive) via scripts/pipeline.py --character <id>.
  5. Export glTF with Draco compression to assets/<id>/webgl/character.glb. Target file size < 6MB per character.

8. Loading and streaming budget

The 3D world must be interactive within 4 seconds on a modern desktop, 8 seconds on mobile. Enforce these budgets:
  • Initial JS bundle for /lounge: < 400KB gzipped
  • First scene render: room geometry + Pauli only. Other characters stream in over the next 5 seconds.
  • Textures: 1024×1024 max on desktop, 512×512 on mobile
  • Total asset budget for the lounge: 40MB desktop, 18MB mobile
  • No character glTF is loaded until the visitor's camera has been idle in Free-look mode for at least 1.5s

9. The rendering aesthetic (design law)

The visual language is Pauli's CHARACTER_CONFIG palette applied to the whole world:

  • Background / ambient: #140F1E deep purple-black
  • Neutral surfaces (walls, ceiling, wood): warm dark grays with a subtle purple tint
  • Accent lighting: #FF6432 fire orange, used for spotlights over Zone B, neon signs in the alley, and rim lighting on speaking characters
  • Gold trims and highlights: #C8AA32
  • Text on screens and holograms: #E6DCFF lavender white

No glassmorphism. No backdrop-blur cards. No gradient text. Every UI overlay uses solid fills. Every 3D material uses PBR with sensible roughness. Bar wood is rough (0.85). Metal (chain, bar rail) is polished (0.15). Windows are refractive glass.

10. Acceptance criteria

  1. Route /lounge renders the 6 zones as described in section 3, dimensions verified with a debug-mode overlay.
  2. All 7 launch avatars load in the correct idle positions and animate their idle loops.
  3. Every event type in section 5 has a scene handler with a matching name in the code.
  4. Voice commands (subsystem 08) reach the nearest avatar and trigger the attentive animation.
  5. Load budgets in section 8 are met in a Lighthouse mobile test.
  6. Rendering aesthetic (section 9) passes a manual review — no glass cards, no gradient text, palette matches.
  7. The WebSocket connection auto-reconnects with exponential backoff (1s, 2s, 4s, 8s, 16s, then 30s).
